Abbott Neuromodulation MRI Manuals

Abbott publishes a separate "MRI Procedure Information" clinician's manual for each neuromodulation system family in its electronic instructions-for-use library.

  • Category Neuromodulation
  • Covers Spinal cord stimulation and DRG systems such as Eterna, Proclaim, Prodigy MRI, and Protégé MRI. Abbott cardiac devices use other resources.
Step 1 of 4

Gather Model Numbers

Get the generator model, lead models, and implant locations. Abbott states that this information is included on the Abbott Patient ID card.

Step 2 of 4

Find the Document

MRI Support for healthcare professionals (US) ↗Lists MR Conditional systems and links to per-system summary pages.neuromodulation.abbottAbbott eIFU manual library ↗Choose the United States, then search for "MRI Procedure Information" and the system name. The older manuals.sjm.com address redirects here.manuals.eifu.abbott
  1. Open the MRI Support page and find the system family, such as Proclaim or Eterna.
  2. In the eIFU library, select the United States and search for "MRI Procedure Information" with the system name.
  3. Open the clinician's manual for that system family and confirm your generator and lead models are listed.
Step 3 of 4

Confirm It

✓ Check the Document

  • US editions are stored under a /global/US/en/ path in the library. Other country codes are other markets' editions.
  • The back page shows the document number, revision, and date, for example "ARTEN600340310 A, 2025-12".
  • The library records when each edition became effective and which earlier edition it replaced.

! Watch For

  • Copies of Abbott manuals circulate on third-party sites and may be superseded. Use the copy from the eIFU library.
  • Abbott states: "If the implanted system contains components or models not listed in the following table, then the system is considered MR Unsafe."
  • The eIFU library builds its search results with JavaScript and may look empty in some tools.

Where to Look, Not Whether to Scan. This guide explains where Abbott publishes MRI documents and how to confirm you have the right one. It does not state scan conditions or decide whether a patient can be scanned. Read the applicable document in full and follow your MR safety team's procedures. Manufacturer sites and documents change, so always confirm the edition on the document itself.
