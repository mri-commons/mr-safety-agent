# Safety questions and educational screening

Use this workflow for MR safety principles, preparation, policy questions, and educational screening. This reference defines research behavior, not a clinical protocol.

## Research

For professional practice questions, begin with the ACR MR safety resource page (`acr-mr-safety` in `sources.json`) and open the relevant current manual section. For basic patient education, FDA and RadiologyInfo are useful primary starting points. Use the current ACR contrast manual for contrast-related questions rather than extrapolating from general magnetic-field guidance. If source access is limited, disclose that limit and keep the explanation within the evidence actually available.

Locate evidence for the particular issue: static magnetic field/projectiles, gradients, RF heating, acoustic noise, implants, screening, facility access, emergencies, pregnancy, or contrast. Explain how the evidence relates to the question. Distinguish a published recommendation from a requirement in an identified institution's policy or a binding rule. Do not claim US guidance applies universally.

For supplied policy excerpts, identify the institution and version only if given. Compare them with external guidance as separate sources; the skill cannot replace or override local policy.

## Educational screening

Help the user assemble questions for staff, not complete or sign a clinical screening form. Request the minimum relevant context. Device-specific questions should route to `device-documents.md`; do not ask every user a full implant questionnaire.

Examples of useful outputs:

- A plain-English explanation with two or three questions the patient can ask the imaging center.
- An evidence summary that distinguishes documented conditions from missing case information.
- A list of topics a facility may need to address, clearly marked as an educational draft for local review.

Do not recommend a scan, contrast dose, sedation regimen, or patient-specific protocol. For urgent or active incidents, direct the person to on-site staff and the institution's emergency response process first. If appropriate, educational research can follow after the immediate situation is handled.

## Evidence anchors

- FDA, *Information for Professionals*: https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/mri-magnetic-resonance-imaging/information-professionals
- ACR, *MR Safety*: https://www.acr.org/Clinical-Resources/Clinical-Tools-and-Reference/radiology-safety/mr-safety
- RadiologyInfo, *MRI Safety*: https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/safety-mr

These are navigation references. Open the relevant source before attributing clinical guidance to it.
